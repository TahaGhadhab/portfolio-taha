How a problem gets solved

Five phases, in order. The order is the point — skipping straight to a fix is how solutions come undone three months later.

  1. 01

    Listen

    Talk to the people inside the problem before touching anything. They already know where it hurts.

  2. 02

    Stabilize

    Put a temporary fix in, sized to how critical the failure is. Buy the time to do the real work properly.

  3. 03

    Decompose

    Break the system into parts. Solve the independent ones first, then the ones tangled together.

  4. 04

    Optimize

    Compare the candidate solutions on quality against cost. Pick with math, not with taste.

  5. 05

    Sustain

    Manage the rollout so it holds together during setup — and keeps holding after handover.

Where the method came from

I grew up somewhere small, where a thing that broke got fixed rather than replaced, and whoever fixed it had to live with the result. That is still how I work.

Industrial engineering gave me the formal tools — decomposition, constraint analysis, optimization under cost. Software and AI gave me somewhere else to point them. The two are the same job: find the part of the system that is actually limiting everything else, and change that part.

I care most about durability. A clever solution that needs me in the room to survive is not a solution.
